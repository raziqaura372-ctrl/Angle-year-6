# TEACHER IMPLEMENTATION GUIDE
## Desert Geometry Expedition (MTES6032 Task 2)

### 1. Pedagogical Rationale & Philosophy
The **Desert Geometry Expedition** is structured around Richard Skemp's theory of **Relational Understanding** (*knowing both what to do and why*). Rather than treating angles as static drawings on paper, students directly manipulate vertex points, ray arms, and polygon structures to observe how mathematical relationships change dynamically.

---

### 2. Formative Assessment & DUNE AI Helper Integration
- **DUNE AI Companion** acts as a Socratic guide. It never gives direct answers, but instead provides a 3-level scaffolding hint:
  - *Level 1:* Visual Clue (highlighting vertices and rays)
  - *Level 2:* Conceptual Hint (reminding angle properties)
  - *Level 3:* Explicit Mathematical Guidance
- **Teacher Dashboard:** Use the Teacher Dashboard tab to monitor completed missions, review student reflection logs, and override difficulty levels (`Level A - Guided`, `Level B - Standard`, `Level C - Challenge`) based on formative needs.

---

### 3. Suggested 30-Minute Micro-Teaching Lesson Structure (Task 3 Preparation)
- **Phase 1: Set Induction & Narrative Hook (5 Mins):** Launch Mission 0. Demonstrate dynamic ray manipulation on the interactive canvas.
- **Phase 2: Guided Dynamic Exploration (10 Mins):** Pairs navigate Mission 2 & 3. Students use the virtual protractor overlay to construct exact gate angles.
- **Phase 3: Polygon Investigation (10 Mins):** Small groups construct 5 and 6-sided polygons on square/isometric grids in Mission 4, testing interior angle sums `(n-2) × 180°`.
- **Phase 4: Closure & Metacognitive Reflection (5 Mins):** Students record entries in the Reflection Journal.
